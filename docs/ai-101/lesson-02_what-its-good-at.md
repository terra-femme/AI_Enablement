# Lesson 2 — What It's Good (and Bad) At
**Time:** 7 min · **Core message:** *Match the task to the tool — use it for language work, not as a source of truth.*

## Objective
The room can sort their own daily tasks into "great fit," "fine with checking," and "don't."

## Demo first (2 min)
Take a real (non-confidential) task from someone in the room — "summarize this long email thread," "turn these bullet points into a polite client update" — and do it live. Then show a bad fit: ask for a specific statistic and show why you'd have to verify it.

## The 3 buckets (rule of 3)
1. **Great fit (language tasks):** drafting, summarizing, rewriting, translating, brainstorming, explaining, formatting.
2. **Fine — *with a human check*:** anything with facts, numbers, names, dates, or that goes to a client.
3. **Don't:** final decisions about people (hiring/firing), anything needing confidential data in an unapproved tool, professional advice presented as the firm's judgment without expert review.

## The anchor analogy
> Use AI like a **word processor with a brain** — fantastic for shaping language. The moment facts or a real decision are involved, *you* are still the professional.

## Talking points / script beats
- "Notice the pattern: it's strongest where there's no single right answer — wording, tone, structure."
- "It's weakest exactly where you'd want to be most careful — facts and decisions. That's not a flaw to fix, it's a line to respect."

## Quick interactive (1 min)
Ask: **"Shout out one task from your job."** Sort each into bucket 1/2/3 live. This makes it real and personal — and it's the seed of their department playbook (Tier 2).

## Common stumble (and the fix)
- "Can't it just do my whole job?" → Reframe: "It removes the boring 70% so you spend time on the 30% only you can do." Productivity, not replacement.

## So what / now what
**Now what:** before using AI, ask "which bucket is this?" If bucket 2, plan to verify. If bucket 3, stop.
