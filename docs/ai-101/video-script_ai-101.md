# AI 101 — Video Script (short-form / recorded version)
### A 6–8 minute video version of the lunch & learn, or split into 6 clips

**Use:** post as one explainer, or chop each scene into a standalone 60–90s clip (LinkedIn/YouTube/internal). Pairs with the Remotion pipeline in `../content-production-toolkit.md`.
**Tone:** warm, plain, zero jargon. Talk to one nervous person, not "an audience."

> Format key: **[ON SCREEN]** = text/graphic cue · **(VO)** = what you say.

---

## Scene 0 — Hook (0:00–0:20)
**[ON SCREEN]** "Your company just gave you AI. Now what?"
**(VO)** "If you just got access to AI at work and feel a mix of excited and terrified — this is for you. In a few minutes you'll know what it actually is, when to use it, and how not to get burned. No tech background needed."

## Scene 1 — What it actually is (0:20–1:40)
**[ON SCREEN]** "It's a brilliant intern. Not a live oracle."
**(VO)** "Here's the one idea that makes you safer than most people: AI doesn't *know* facts — it predicts helpful-sounding language. It learned from data up to a cutoff date, so it's not live unless a tool gives it the web. And it can be *confidently wrong* — making up names, numbers, even sources. Think of it as a brilliant new intern who's been on a desert island: fast and well-read, but you check its work."
**[ON SCREEN]** ❌ live · ❌ search engine · ❌ confident = correct

## Scene 2 — What it's good at (1:40–3:00)
**[ON SCREEN]** Three buckets: Great fit / Check it / Don't.
**(VO)** "Match the task to the tool. It's *great* at language work — drafting, summarizing, rewriting, translating, brainstorming. It's fine for anything with facts *as long as a human checks*. And don't use it for final decisions about people, or for confidential data in tools you're not sure about. It removes the boring 70% so you do the 30% only you can."

## Scene 3 — How to ask (3:00–4:30)
**[ON SCREEN]** "CREF: Context · Role · Example · Format"
**(VO)** "Better input, better output. Don't say 'write an email.' Say: here's the situation *(context)*, act like a calm account manager *(role)*, here's our usual tone *(example)*, give me three short paragraphs *(format)*. Brief it like you'd brief a person — and if it's off, just tell it what to fix. It's a conversation."
**[ON SCREEN]** before/after email side-by-side

## Scene 4 — Staying safe (4:30–6:00)
**[ON SCREEN]** "Verify. And the stranger test."
**(VO)** "Two habits. One: verify before you send — you're responsible for anything you submit. Two: the stranger test — if you couldn't email it to a stranger, don't paste it into AI you're not sure about. Use only your company-approved tool. And heads up — AI makes scams flawless now, even fake voices of your boss. Any request for money, data, or access? Confirm it through a second channel."

## Scene 5 — Saving your work (6:00–7:00)
**[ON SCREEN]** "Projects + Version History"
**(VO)** "You don't need any developer tools. Group your chats in Projects. Save finished work to your team drive — and that drive's 'Version History' is your undo-button across time. Stop saving to your desktop."

## Scene 6 — Close (7:00–7:40)
**[ON SCREEN]** "Your 3 habits: Verify · Stranger test · Save it home."
**(VO)** "That's it. Verify before you send. Don't paste what you couldn't email a stranger. Save your work home. Do those three and you're already ahead of most people using AI. Pick one real task this week and try it."
**[ON SCREEN]** [your handle / "AI Enablement & Governance"]
