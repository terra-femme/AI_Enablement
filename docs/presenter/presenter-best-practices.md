# Presenter Best Practices
### For your first time teaching AI *and* your first time public speaking + giving advice

Read this the night before and the hour before. It's written for the specific fears you named:
not feeling 100% expert, nervous speaking, nervous giving advice.

---

## 1. The mindset that fixes the "I'm not an expert" fear
You don't need to be an expert. **You need to be one solid step ahead of the room and honest about the rest.** Your audience thought AI was live and didn't know what a model is — you already know more than enough to help them *today*.

- Reframe your role: you're not "the AI genius." You're **the guide who makes this safe and usable.** That's a different, more valuable job.
- Your real credentials: sales, office management, finance/compliance. You speak *business*, not just tech. Most AI trainers can't. **Lead with that.**
- "Expert" is a trap. The goal is **useful + trustworthy**, not omniscient.

## 2. The single most trust-building sentence you can say
> **"Great question — I don't want to guess. Let me confirm that and follow up."**

Saying this *builds* authority; bluffing destroys it. The fastest way to lose a room is to be caught making something up — exactly the thing you're teaching them not to do with AI. Model the behavior. Keep a "follow-up list" visible and actually follow up — that one habit makes you look more professional than any slick answer.

## 3. Beat stage fright with structure, not willpower
- **Over-prepare the first 2 minutes.** Memorize your opening word-for-word. Nerves are worst at the start; a locked-in open carries you.
- **Demo early.** Doing a live thing pulls focus off *you* and onto the screen. Lesson 1 starts with a demo for this reason.
- **Notes are allowed.** Use the lesson files / a printed cheat sheet. Pros use notes. It reads as prepared, not unsure.
- **Slow down 20%.** Nervous = fast. Pause after each key point — silence feels long to you, normal to them.
- **One person at a time.** Make eye contact with one friendly face per sentence. It turns "a crowd" into "a chat."

## 4. Teach like a deep learner who learned to cut (your specific trap)
You over-include. Defenses:
- **One idea per lesson. Rule of 3. Analogy first.** (Already built into every lesson file — trust the structure.)
- Write the deep version for *yourself* (your knowledge base), then **say only the 30%.** The depth shows up in how calmly you handle questions, not in the slides.
- When you feel the urge to add a 4th point: that's the next session, not this slide.
- **Demo before theory, every time.** Show it work, *then* explain. Deep learners do the reverse and lose people.

## 5. Giving advice responsibly (the part that protects you)
You're advising a real business — be useful *and* careful:
- **Separate fact from opinion out loud:** "This is how the tool works" vs. "Here's what I'd recommend."
- **Stay in your lane.** For legal, HR, or compliance specifics, say "confirm with your legal/HR team." (Your AUP template does this too.) This isn't weakness — it's professionalism.
- **Don't promise outcomes** ("this will save 10 hours"). Promise *approach* ("teams that do X usually find Y").
- **Verify before you assert** — especially product specifics (models, enterprise data terms, retention). See `presenter-knowledge-base.md`. Getting one fact wrong in public costs more than saying "let me confirm."
- **No real client/PHI data in demos — ever.** Use fake examples. You're modeling the rule you're teaching.

## 6. Handling hard / hostile questions
- **The skeptic** ("this is just hype"): agree partly — "a lot *is* hype, which is why we focus on what actually works and what's risky."
- **The expert in the room** (a dev who knows more): hand them respect — "you clearly know this deeply — anything you'd add for the team?" Turn them into an ally, not a rival.
- **The fearful** ("it'll take our jobs"): validate, then reframe to tasks-not-jobs. Don't dismiss the fear.
- **The unanswerable:** the follow-up sentence from §2. Always.

## 7. Pre-session checklist
- [ ] Opening 2 minutes memorized
- [ ] Approved tool logged in; **fake** demo doc ready; internet/backup plan
- [ ] Cheat sheet (`../ai-101/qa_common-questions.md`) open
- [ ] Knowledge base skimmed (`presenter-knowledge-base.md`) so hard Qs don't rattle you
- [ ] Leave-behind 1-pager printed/linked
- [ ] Exit poll ready (your case-study data)
- [ ] Water. Phone on silent. Arrive early to test the screen.

## 8. After the session (turn it into career fuel)
- Send the follow-up answers you promised (within 24h — huge trust signal).
- Collect one quote/testimonial.
- Save the exit-poll numbers — "raised confidence from X to Y across N people" is a **resume line and a sales pitch.**
- Write down the 3 questions you couldn't answer → those become your next study topics and content pieces.
