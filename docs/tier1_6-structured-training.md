# Tier 1.6 — Structured Training: Using Claude Academy In A Rollout
### Free vendor courses now exist. Here's how to fold them in without making yourself redundant.

**Audience:** rollout owners, L&D, champions — and the practitioner planning an engagement
**Framing for the buyer:** "You don't have to invent AI training from scratch. The vendor gives away a solid foundation. What you're buying from me is everything that foundation can't cover — your data, your rules, your rollout."

**Teaching rules (per venture standard):** one idea per asset, demo-before-theory, rule of 3, analogy-first. Each numbered sub-module below = one standalone content asset (video / one-pager / slide).

> Verified **2026-08-26** at [academy.claude.com](https://academy.claude.com). Course catalogues change — re-check names, lesson counts and durations before quoting them in a proposal.

## 1.6.0 — The single idea (the hook)

Most organizations roll out AI by buying licences and sending an announcement email. Then they're surprised when usage flatlines at 15% and the people using it are mostly writing haikus.

The missing piece isn't access. It's **structured practice with an assessment at the end** — the same thing that makes any other professional tool stick.

That used to be expensive to build. It isn't any more: **Claude Academy** is free, and it covers the foundation layer properly.

> **Analogy:** giving someone a licence is handing them car keys. A course is driving lessons. Almost every failed AI rollout is a fleet of keys and nobody who took a lesson.

**So what / now what:** stop building beginner content. Assign it, then spend your budget on the things a vendor course structurally cannot teach — covered in 1.6.3.

## 1.6.2 — The 4D framework, and why it matters to us

*AI Fluency* teaches four disciplines — **Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence**:

- **Delegation** — deciding what to hand to AI and what to keep
- **Description** — communicating the task well
- **Discernment** — judging the output rather than accepting it
- **Diligence** — working ethically and safely

Note the fourth one. **Diligence is governance**, taught to end users as a personal skill rather than an org policy. That's a gift: it means the vendor is already priming your learners with the instinct that Tier 3 formalises.

> **Analogy:** the course teaches personal hygiene. Governance is the public health system. You need both, and the first makes the second far easier to sell.

**So what / now what:** align vocabulary deliberately. If learners meet "discernment" in Claude Academy, use that same word in your verification-habit module (Tier 1) instead of inventing a competing term. Curriculum that reinforces the vendor's language feels coherent; curriculum that fights it feels like two competing systems and learners drop one.

## 1.6.3 — What vendor training structurally cannot do (the honest value proposition)

This is the module that protects the engagement, and it should be said plainly to buyers rather than hidden.

Claude Academy teaches **the tool, brilliantly**. It cannot teach:

1. **Your data boundaries.** No vendor knows which of your systems holds regulated data, or what your DPA permits. That's Tier 3.2, and it has to be written by someone who knows your business.
2. **Your rules and accountability.** Acceptable-use policy, data classification, who signs off on a tier-3 use case, what happens when someone breaks it. Tier 3.
3. **Vendor-neutral judgment.** A vendor course will not tell you when a task doesn't need AI, when a cheaper model suffices, or when a competitor fits better. That independence is a service you sell.
4. **Role-specific workflows in your context.** "Prompting for HR" in the abstract is worth little next to "here's how we screen applicants, and here's the line we don't cross" — which needs Tier 5.1 and your actual process.
5. **Rollout, adoption and measurement.** Champions, metrics, the 30/60/90. Tier 4. Nobody's course knows your org chart.

> **Analogy:** the manufacturer teaches you to operate the machine. They can't write your factory's safety procedures, decide which shifts run it, or tell you whether you needed the machine at all.

**So what / now what:** position vendor training as **pre-work**, not competition. "Everyone completes Claude 101 and AI Fluency before our first session" raises the floor of the room for free and lets you spend paid hours where only you can add value. It also makes your proposals more credible — a consultant who says "don't pay me for the basics, the vendor gives those away" is one buyers trust with the harder questions.

## 1.6.4 — Certification, and what it signals

Anthropic runs a certification track — the **Claude Certified Architect, Foundations** exam launched for solution architects building production applications, with further certifications for sellers, architects and developers added later in 2026. As of the 2026 Claude Partner Network announcement, **more than 36,000 consultants across 1,300+ organizations** had been certified since March.

Two implications, depending on which side of the table you're on:

- **For practitioners:** certification is becoming a visible credential in this market, and 36,000 people holding one sets a baseline. Worth tracking against the AIGP and other governance credentials — they signal different things. A vendor certification says *"I can build with this tool."* A governance credential says *"I can tell you whether you should."*
- **For buying organizations:** when evaluating an AI consultant, certification is a reasonable question. It is also not sufficient on its own — the five gaps in 1.6.3 are exactly what a tool certification doesn't cover.

**So what / now what:** the honest pitch is *both*. Tool fluency proves you can build it; governance credentials prove you know what shouldn't be built. Most buyers are only being offered the first.

## 1.6.5 — Dropping it into the syllabus

Suggested sequencing for a real engagement:

| Stage | What happens | Who delivers |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-work** | Claude 101 + AI Fluency assigned, ~7 hrs, before kickoff | Vendor (free) |
| **Session 1** | Tier 0 myth-busting + Tier 1 verification habit, calibrated to what pre-work already covered | You |
| **Session 2** | Tier 3.1 regulation + Tier 3.2 data drawers — *their* data, *their* obligations | You |
| **Session 3** | Tier 2 role playbooks, using their actual workflows | You |
| **Ongoing** | Tier 4 rollout, champions, measurement; Cowork course for teams ready to delegate multi-step work | Both |

> **The rule of 3 for rollout owners:** assign the free foundation, teach the parts that are specific to you, and measure whether anything changed.

**So what / now what:** an engagement structured this way costs the client less and delivers more, which is a better story than pretending the free courses don't exist. They will find them anyway.

## Delivery notes

- **Check the catalogue before every proposal.** Course names, lesson counts and durations move. Quoting a course that's been renamed is a small error that reads as a big one.
- **Don't oversell the pre-work completion rate.** Assigned optional training gets finished by maybe half a room. Build the session so it works for people who skipped it, and reward the ones who didn't.
- **This module doubles as a trust move.** Volunteering that a free alternative covers the basics is disarming, and it makes everything you *do* charge for look deliberate rather than padded.
